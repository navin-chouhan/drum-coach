1 · The grip, moving: from the side and from above

Both views play the same full strokes at the lesson's tempo. From the side: the stick pivots between the thumb and the index finger while the wrist turns; the back fingers open a little on the way up and close loosely on the way down. From above: where the stick sits in the hand, both hands the same, the butt end showing behind the hand; the shadow on the pad shows how high each tip is. ✗ Wrong plays the beginner's mistake in each view. Faint "ghost" sticks show the motion; the pad flashes and ripples on every hit.
From the side
From above

Watch for

  • The stick rocks in the hand at one point: thumb pad + first joint of the index finger, a third up from the end.
  • The wrist turns; the forearm hardly moves.
  • A small gap between the palm and the stick: the back fingers guide, they never squeeze.
  • From above: back of the hand up, thumbs along the sides, both hands the same, the sticks in a V.

The mistakes (✗ Wrong)

Side: a fist. Thumb on top and every finger clamped: the stick can't bounce, it stays on the pad, and the whole arm has to lift it. Loud, slow, and your forearm burns in minutes.

Above: holding the very end. No butt end behind the hand, so there's no balance point: the stick feels heavy and dies on the pad.

2 · Kick: heel down and heel up

A real pedal this time (footboard, chain, cam, spring, felt beater) in front of your MPS-150 kick pad, a shoe instead of a triangle, and a leg that bends at the hip, knee and ankle. The yellow ring marks what the foot pivots on. Sound waves leave the pad on a good stroke; a buried beater gets a short dull "thud".
Heel down

Heel down: the ankle plays

Watch for

    The mistake (✗ Wrong)

    Burying: the foot stays pressed after the note, so the beater rests on the pad and chokes the sound. Let the pedal push your foot back up.

    In the app

    "How it moves" gets these as three movements: Grip (side), Grip (above) and the new Kick, drawn from the same keyframes engine as the full stroke, so they run at the lesson's tempo, in slow motion, mirrored for left-handed players, and with the coach speaking the cue words (D15). The grip still picture is retired. Posture, singles and doubles lessons open "Grip (side)"; kick lessons open "Kick" on the stage that matches the lesson (heel down for soft grooves, heel up from the money beat on).
